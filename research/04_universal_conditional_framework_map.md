# Universal vs. Conditional Framework Map
**Universal Professional Scriptwriting Skill — Phase 5 Output**
**Last updated:** 2026-10-14

## Purpose
Classify every extracted finding by applicability scope: what holds universally across all scriptwriting, what is format-specific, category-specific, niche-specific, or context-dependent. This prevents overgeneralization and ensures the eventual skill applies the right knowledge in the right context.

---

## Classification Key

| Code | Scope | Definition | Example |
|------|-------|------------|---------|
| **U** | Universal | Applies to all professional scriptwriting regardless of format, genre, or medium | "One idea per sentence" |
| **FS** | Format-Specific | Applies within a specific format (video, audio, stage, interactive) | "Visual changes every 3–5 seconds" |
| **CS** | Category-Specific | Applies within a category (fiction, nonfiction, educational, commercial) | "Try/fail escalation" |
| **NS** | Niche-Specific | Applies to a narrow niche (mystery, romance, YouTube explainer) | "Fair-play clue distribution" |
| **CD** | Context-Dependent | Applies conditionally based on audience, purpose, or constraints | "Expertise reversal effect" |
| **F** | Framework | Structural model offered as one option among alternatives, not a universal rule | "Save the Cat 15 beats" |

### Framework Selection Guidance (synthesized from sources)

The sources collectively suggest these selection criteria:
1. **Story length** — Shorter forms need fewer beats (7-point or MICE). Longer forms can support 22–36 beats.
2. **Character vs. plot emphasis** — Character-driven → Truby or Crucible. Plot-driven → Save the Cat or Hero's Journey.
3. **Genre conventions** — Commercial genre → Save the Cat. Literary → Truby. Epic/fantasy → Crucible or Hero's Journey.
4. **Organic vs. formulaic** — Truby and Crucible resist beat-by-beat prescription. Save the Cat and Hero's Journey are more prescriptive.
5. **Discovery vs. planning** — Discovery drafters → MICE or Five Commandments (scene-level). Outliners → Snowflake or beat sheets.
